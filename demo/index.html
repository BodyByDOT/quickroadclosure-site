Line-of-sight warnings

Catch it on the map. Not at the hill.

Closures planned on paper can fail in the field when hills, curves or grown-up brush hide the signs at that speed.

Concept mock-up · not to scale

  • Checks sight lines as you build to signs, tapers and flagger stations: terrain, curves and obstructions vs. the distance the speed requires (MUTCD Part 6 + state rules).
  • Warns during planning, suggests fixesMove signs upstreamExtend advance warningAdd flagger / pilot carLower speed
  • Flags stale imagery by capture date (street-view can be years old); crews confirm or correct it from the field and the change syncs to everyone.
  • Alert levels you control: imagery age threshold, sensitivity, warn vs. block. An org admin can set a minimum users can't go below.

Live updates · story

Change it once. Everyone sees it.

Concept mock-up
  1. 1Spot it
  2. 2Fix it in the app
  3. 3Everyone sees it
  1. On an active closure, a crew member sees that a sign needs to move.
  2. Right there, they drag the sign to its new spot in the app and push the update.
  3. The update goes out to everyone: the planner's PC in the office refreshes…
  4. …and a worker at the far end gets a "Closure updated" alert, opens it and sees the change live.
Inventory & bidding

Know what you have. Bid what it costs.

  • Optional, tied to your company template: vehicles, crash attenuator / protection trucks, signs and stands, barrels and cones, arrow boards, employees (names and count) and more.
  • Running totals across every closure: allocation tracked across all logins and scheduled closures, up to worldwide, for logistics planning.
  • Item costs built in: estimate what a closure will cost when bidding on projects.
  • Fleet maintenance link (optional): equipment out for repair shows as in the shop with an expected return, and is left out of available inventory for closures and bids.
